# Oxygen-Vacancy Effects in Room-Temperature Ferromagnetism of ZnO Thin Films

A. Kumar, M. Chen — Department of Physics, Example University.
Corresponding author: a.kumar@example.edu

## Abstract

Thin films of ZnO were grown by pulsed laser deposition. X-ray
diffraction shows wurtzite structure. Magnetic measurements reveal weak
ferromagnetism at room temperature. We propose oxygen vacancies mediate
the magnetic ordering.

## Introduction

Dilute magnetic oxides have been investigated extensively (Kumar and
Chen, 2019). Room-temperature ferromagnetism in ZnO remains debated
(Chen, 2020).

## Experimental Section

Thin films of ZnO were deposited on sapphire substrates by pulsed laser
deposition. A KrF excimer laser (248 nm) was used at 2 J/cm2. The
chamber base pressure was 1e-6 Torr.

## Results and Discussion

The co-processed samples showed saturation magnetization of 0.8 emu/g.
Oxygen vacancies were quantiﬁed by photoluminescence. The eﬀect of
deposition temperature was studied; the ca ﬁlms were characterized by
AFM.

## Conclusions

Oxygen vacancies mediate room-temperature ferromagnetism in ZnO thin
films grown under the described conditions.
