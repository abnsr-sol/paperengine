# Testing Human Memory Consolidation Across Sleep Cycles

J. Ramirez, K. Osei — Department of Psychology, Example University.
Corresponding author: j.ramirez@example.edu

## Abstract

We examined how sleep deprivation affects memory consolidation using a
within-subjects design with 32 participants. Participants completed a
word-pair recall task after normal sleep and after sleep deprivation.
Recall accuracy dropped significantly after deprivation (d = 0.74).
Findings suggest sleep plays a causal role in consolidating declarative
memories.

## Introduction

Prior work shows sleep supports memory consolidation (Rasch and Born,
2013). Sleep deprivation impairs hippocampal function (Yoo et al.,
2007). However, few studies isolate declarative recall from procedural
learning (Walker, 2017).

## Methods

Thirty-two undergraduate volunteers (18 female, mean age 20.4) took part
for course credit. Participants gave written informed consent. The
protocol was approved by the Example University IRB (Protocol #2024-118).
Each participant completed two sessions: one after a normal night's sleep
and one after 24 hours of sleep deprivation, in counterbalanced order.

## Results

Recall accuracy fell from 71.2% (SD = 9.8) after normal sleep to 58.6%
(SD = 11.3) after deprivation, t(31) = 4.12, p < 0.001. The effect was
stable across genders.

## Discussion

Results are consistent with the active-consolidation hypothesis (Rasch
and Born, 2013). Sleep loss selectively impaired declarative recall,
mirroring (Yoo et al., 2007). Limitations include the young sample and
single-deprivation dose.

## Conclusion

Sleep deprivation causally impairs declarative memory consolidation in
young adults.
