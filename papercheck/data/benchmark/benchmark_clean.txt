# Effects of Adaptive Load Balancing on Distributed Throughput

A. Author, B. Author, C. Author — Department of Computer Science, Example
University. Corresponding author: a.author@example.edu

## Abstract

We evaluated an adaptive load-balancing algorithm across three datacenter
clusters. In a randomized experiment with 120 server nodes, the algorithm
improved median throughput by 18% (95% CI 12% to 24%; Cohen's d = 0.61).
Normality was assessed with Shapiro-Wilk tests; group comparisons used
Welch's t-tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction for the six pre-registered
comparisons. All data and code are available at https://github.com/example/alb.

## Introduction

Distributed systems must balance load under heterogeneous demand. Prior work
established that static partitioning underutilizes capacity during bursts
[1] and that naive rebalancing oscillates under churn [2]. We contribute an
adaptive controller with hysteresis and evaluate it under pre-registered
hypotheses. The remainder of this paper describes the methods, results,
limitations, and conclusions.

## Methods

We randomized 120 nodes across three clusters (n = 40 per cluster) using
block randomization with a fixed seed (42). A priori power analysis
(G*Power 3.1, f = 0.25, alpha = 0.05, power = 0.80) indicated 34 nodes per
group were sufficient; we enrolled 40 to allow for attrition. Throughput was
measured every 60 seconds for 24 hours. Shapiro-Wilk tests assessed
normality; Levene's test assessed homoscedasticity. Six pre-registered
comparisons were corrected using Holm-Bonferroni. The study protocol was
approved by our institutional review board (IRB-2024-0192). The dataset
(2.1 GB) and analysis code (Python 3.11, pinned requirements) are archived
at Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.9999999) under a CC-BY 4.0 license.

## Results

Median throughput improved 18% in the adaptive condition versus static
partitioning (Welch's t(78) = 3.42, p = .001, d = 0.61, 95% CI on the mean
difference 12% to 24%). Tail latency (p99) was unchanged (p = .31). Across
the six pre-registered comparisons, all corrected p-values remained
significant except the burst-duration interaction (corrected p = .08).
Figure 1 shows the throughput distributions; Table 1 reports per-cluster
statistics.

## Limitations

Our evaluation covers three clusters of one cloud provider; results may not
transfer to on-premises hardware. The 24-hour window excludes seasonal
patterns. The controller's hysteresis parameters were tuned on a pilot
cluster excluded from analysis, which may introduce optimism.

## Conclusion

Adaptive load balancing with hysteresis improved median throughput by 18%
under the studied conditions. Future work should evaluate federated
deployments and adversarial traffic mixes.
