# Effects of a Mindfulness App on Perceived Stress in University Students: A Pilot Study

O. Author, Z. Author — Department of Psychology, Example University.
Corresponding author: o.author@example.edu

## Abstract

We evaluated an eight-week mindfulness app in a randomized controlled
trial with 60 university students. Perceived stress improved versus the
waitlist control. Human subjects were recruited from campus
announcements.

## Introduction

Perceived stress is prevalent among students [1]. Mindfulness
interventions show promise [2] [3]. We tested a low-cost app delivery
model.

## Methods

Sixty students aged 18-25 were randomly assigned to the app or waitlist.
Perceived stress scales were administered at baseline and eight weeks.
Participants used personal smartphones for daily exercises.

## Results

Stress scores decreased by 2.1 points in the intervention arm versus
0.3 in controls (p = 0.03). Adherence was 71% at week eight.

## Discussion

App-delivered mindfulness reduced perceived stress in this pilot.
Findings align with meta-analytic estimates [2]. Effects on clinical
outcomes require further study.

## Limitations

Waitlist design cannot blind participants; self-report outcomes are
subject to expectancy effects; the sample was from a single campus.
