# Student Adoption of a Version-Control Workflow in a Software Engineering Course

C. Author, H. Author — Faculty of Engineering, Example University.
Corresponding author: c.author@example.edu

## Abstract

We studied student adoption of a version-control workflow across two
course cohorts. Survey data and repository analytics were combined to
characterize usage patterns. Adoption increased steadily over the
semester and correlated with project grades.

## Introduction

Version control is a core professional skill [1] [2]. Prior work
reports mixed adoption in coursework [3]. We examined adoption
trajectories using repository event logs.

## Methods

Two cohorts (N = 74 total) used the workflow across a 12-week project.
We extracted commit frequency from repository logs and administered an
end-of-semester questionnaire. Grades were obtained from course records
after the teaching period.

## Results

Median commits per student rose from 6 in weeks 1-4 to 19 in weeks 9-12.
Adoption correlated with project grades. Figures 1 and 3 summarize the
trajectories; Table 4 lists per-cohort statistics. The improvement was
echoed in the exit questionnaire [4].

## Discussion

Adoption grew with scaffolded instruction, consistent with prior
findings [2]. The observational design cannot separate skill effects
from engagement effects. Grade correlation should be read cautiously.

## Limitations

Single institution, two cohorts, and self-reported survey items limit
generalizability. Log data cannot capture local uncommitted work.
