# Decision Support with Pre-Validated Clinical Models: A Prospective Multicenter RCT Protocol Analysis

E. Author, U. Author — Academic Medical Center, Example University.
Corresponding author: e.author@example.edu

## Abstract

We report the planned and executed evaluation of a pre-validated
clinical prediction model deployed as decision support in a randomized
controlled trial across four hospitals. The protocol was registered
(NCT04567890), the model was previously externally validated in two
independent cohorts, and all deployment-site variables were identical
to those in the validation cohorts.

## Introduction

Clinical prediction models frequently degrade after deployment because
input pipelines differ from validation settings [1] [2]. TRIPOD
reporting and prior external validation mitigate this risk [3]. We
describe a trial comparing the pre-validated model against usual care
with outcome adjudication by a blinded committee.

## Methods

A priori sample-size calculation targeted 640 participants to detect a
risk ratio of 0.85 with 80% power at alpha 0.05. Participant flow from
enrollment to analysis is summarized in the participant flow diagram
(Figure 1). The model used five candidate predictors identical to its
published specification; no variables were derived from the deployment
data. The trial was registered before the first enrollment. A blinded
adjudication committee classified outcomes against predefined
definitions. Hospitals were randomly assigned to immediate or delayed
deployment by a statistician independent of care delivery, using
block randomization. The ethics board approved the protocol;
participants provided informed consent.

## Results

Enrollment completed with 640 participants across four sites. Model
inputs matched validation specifications at all sites. The adjudicated
primary outcome occurred in 11.9% versus 12.8% of participants
(risk ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.21). No site-level drift in predictor
distributions was detected during the trial window.

## Discussion

Deployment of a pre-validated model with unchanged inputs showed no
signal of performance drift within the trial window. The result should
not be generalized to models without prior external validation.
Outcome adjudication was blinded but treating clinicians were aware of
allocation.

## Limitations

Six-month follow-up may miss longer-horizon drift; four hospitals
within one network limit site heterogeneity; the model itself was not
retrained during the trial.

## Conclusion

Deploying a pre-validated clinical prediction model with unchanged
inputs showed no drift signal within the trial window, supporting
careful-deployment designs over retraining by default.
