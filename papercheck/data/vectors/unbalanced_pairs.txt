# Heart-Rate Variability During Controlled Breathing in Two Posture Groups: A Cross-Over Study

A. Author, W. Author — Cardiovascular Research Lab, Example University.
Corresponding author: a.author@example.edu

## Abstract

We compared heart-rate variability (HRV) during paced breathing between
two seated postures in a randomized cross-over design with 20 healthy
volunteers. Participants were randomly assigned to posture sequence.
The study was approved by the institutional ethics board.

## Introduction

HRV is a non-invasive marker of autonomic function [1]. Posture and
breathing rate strongly influence HRV indices [2]. Standardizing
recording conditions is therefore essential [3].

## Methods

Twenty healthy adults (10 per posture sequence) completed both
conditions in randomized order with a 48-hour washout. ECG was recorded
for 5 minutes per condition during 6 breaths/min paced breathing. The
ethics board approved the protocol; volunteers gave informed consent.
Time-domain and frequency-domain HRV indices were computed per
condition.

## Results

During paced breathing, RMSSD was M = 58, SD = 2, n = 10 in the
supine-like posture versus M = 22, SD = 44, n = 10 upright. The
between-posture difference in dispersion was marked, with one condition
showing near-zero spread and the other roughly twenty-fold greater.
Low-frequency power differed accordingly between the two conditions.

## Discussion

The large difference in short-term variability indices between postures
underscores the need for strict standardization. Findings are consistent
with autonomic shift literature [2]. The cross-over design controls for
between-subject variance but sequence effects cannot be fully excluded.

## Limitations

Small sample, healthy young volunteers, and single laboratory setting.
