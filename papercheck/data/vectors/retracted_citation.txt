# Effects of a Structured Warm-Up Programme on Adolescent Sprint Performance

M. Author, K. Author — Faculty of Sport Science, Example University.
Corresponding author: m.author@example.edu

## Abstract

Wakefield et al. reported that 8 weeks of neuromuscular warm-up reduced
non-contact injuries in adolescent athletes. We replicated the design in
a two-arm randomized controlled trial across 12 schools. The programme
improved 20 m sprint times and reduced injury incidence versus the
standard warm-up. Outcomes were assessed by blinded assessors.

## Introduction

Injury prevention programmes in youth sport are widely recommended [1]
[2]. The original neuromuscular warm-up trial demonstrated large effects
in school settings [3]. However, replication evidence is limited. We
therefore tested the programme in a new cohort, pre-registering the
primary outcome.

## Methods

Twelve schools were randomly assigned to the intervention or control arm
by an independent statistician using sealed opaque envelopes. Eligible
athletes were aged 14 to 16 with no lower-limb injury in the prior six
months. The trial was approved by the university ethics committee
(IRB-2023-114) and informed consent was obtained from guardians. Blinded
assessors measured 20 m sprint time at baseline and 8 weeks.

## Results

Sprint times improved by 0.08 s (95% CI -0.12 to 0.28; p = 0.42) in the
intervention arm versus control. Injury incidence was 12% versus 15%
(RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.61 to 1.05).

## Discussion

Our replication found no statistically significant sprint benefit,
contrasting with the original trial [3]. Differences in coaching
fidelity may explain the discrepancy. The study was limited by a
moderately sized sample and a single season of follow-up.

## Limitations

The cohort was regional, assessors were blinded but coaches were not,
and one school withdrew mid-season. Residual confounding by training
volume outside the programme cannot be excluded.
