# Baseline Characteristics and Perioperative Outcomes of a Novel Anesthetic Regimen: A Randomized Trial

B. Author, I. Author — Department of Anesthesiology, Example University.
Corresponding author: b.author@example.edu

## Abstract

In a randomized double-blind controlled trial, 180 surgical patients
were randomly assigned to the novel anesthetic regimen or standard care.
Baseline characteristics were comparable and perioperative recovery
scores favored the intervention. The trial was approved by the hospital
ethics committee and registered before enrollment.

## Introduction

Fast-track anesthesia protocols aim to shorten recovery room stays [1]
[2]. Evidence in elective abdominal surgery is limited [3]. We conducted
a randomized trial comparing the novel regimen with standard care.

## Methods

Patients scheduled for elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy were
randomly allocated 1:1 using computer-generated codes with concealed
allocation. The outcome assessor was blinded to group assignment. Written
informed consent was obtained from all participants. Baseline variables
were recorded before induction of anesthesia.

## Results

Baseline characteristics: age 41.6 (12.4) vs 42.1 (11.9) years; weight
74.3 (13.1) vs 73.8 (12.7) kg; height 169.8 (8.9) vs 170.2 (9.1) cm;
BMI 25.8 (3.4) vs 25.6 (3.3); duration of surgery 78.4 (18.2) vs 79.1
(17.8) min; ASA score distribution similar across groups; baseline pain
score 3.2 (1.4) vs 3.1 (1.5); preoperative hemoglobin 13.4 (1.2) vs
13.5 (1.3) g/dL; baseline heart rate 72.6 (9.8) vs 73.0 (10.2) bpm;
baseline systolic pressure 124.7 (11.3) vs 125.1 (11.8) mmHg; recovery
room stay 41.9 (12.6) vs 42.4 (13.0) min. Recovery scores at 2 hours
favored the intervention arm.

## Discussion

Baseline comparability supports the internal validity of the
randomization. Recovery benefits were modest. Generalizability is
limited to elective laparoscopic surgery at a single center.

## Limitations

Single center, unblinded surgeons, and a homogeneous elective population
restrict generalizability.
