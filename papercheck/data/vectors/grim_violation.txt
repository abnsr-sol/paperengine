# Gamified Micro-Learning and Retention in Introductory Statistics: A Randomized Classroom Trial

S. Author, P. Author — Department of Education, Example University.
Corresponding author: s.author@example.edu

## Abstract

In a randomized controlled trial across four classrooms, a gamified
micro-learning module was compared with standard tutorials on quiz
retention. The protocol was registered (NCT04123456) and the study
approved by the institutional review board.

## Introduction

Gamification is reported to increase engagement in statistics education
[1] [2]. Effects on durable retention are less studied. We conducted a
pragmatic classroom randomized trial.

## Methods

Four classrooms (N = 96 students, ages 18-24) were randomly assigned to
gamified modules or standard tutorials for three weeks. The primary
outcome was retention quiz score at two weeks. Students provided informed
consent; the trial was prospectively registered. Blinded graders scored
quizzes against a fixed rubric.

## Results

A total of N = 48 students were analyzed across the four classrooms.
Mean retention score in the intervention arm was M = 5.37 with SD = 1.2
on the integer-correct count (0-10 range) derived from 10 discrete
multiple-choice items scored right or wrong per student. The control
arm mean was 4.90 (SD 1.1). Because each item was scored dichotomously
and summed, scores are whole numbers; a mean of 5.37 over 48 students
with 10 items should be verified against the raw item data.

## Discussion

No statistically significant difference in retention was found. The
result contrasts with engagement-only effects reported elsewhere [2].
Interpretation is limited to short-term retention in introductory
settings.

## Limitations

Short intervention window, single institution, and novelty effects may
mask longer-term differences.
