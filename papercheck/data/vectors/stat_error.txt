# Association Between a Habitual Sleep Index and Metabolic Score in Shift Workers

R. Author, D. Author — School of Public Health, Example University.
Corresponding author: r.author@example.edu

## Abstract

A cross-sectional study of 412 shift workers examined the association
between a composite sleep index and a metabolic risk score. Statistical
analysis used chi-square tests for categorical associations. Higher sleep
irregularity was associated with adverse metabolic profiles in women but
not men.

## Introduction

Shift work disrupts circadian alignment and is associated with
cardiometabolic risk [1] [2]. Prior cohort analyses report sex-specific
associations [3]. We analyzed baseline data from an occupational cohort.

## Methods

Participants (N = 412) completed validated questionnaires. The exposure
was a composite habitual sleep index; the outcome a continuous metabolic
score dichotomized at cohort median. Associations were tested with
chi-square tests of independence; alpha 0.05. The occupational cohort
study was approved by the regional ethics board and participants gave
informed consent.

## Results

The sleep index was dichotomized into irregular versus regular sleepers
(178 vs 234 participants). Irregular sleepers more often had adverse
metabolic profiles: chi-square(4) = 14.87, p = 0.005. Sex-stratified
analyses are shown in Table 2. A companion test of dose-response was
non-significant despite a large observed effect: chi2(3) = 18.42,
p = 0.45, which is inconsistent with the reported effect magnitude.

## Discussion

Our findings align with prior observational evidence linking irregular
sleep with metabolic risk [3]. Causal inference is not warranted given
the cross-sectional design; reverse causality is plausible. Residual
confounding by occupational role remains possible.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional design precludes causal claims; the sleep index was
self-reported; the cohort is predominantly one occupational sector.
