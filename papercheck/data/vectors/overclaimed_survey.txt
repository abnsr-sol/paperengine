# Remote Work Arrangements and Employee Well-Being: A Decisive Causal Analysis

L. Author, T. Author — Business School, Example University.
Corresponding author: l.author@example.edu

## Abstract

This conclusive study proves that remote work arrangements cause higher
employee well-being. Our survey establishes the definitive relationship
beyond any doubt. Organizations should immediately adopt remote-first
policies based on these results.

## Introduction

Remote work expanded dramatically after 2020 [1] [2]. Prior studies
suggest associations with satisfaction [3]. This study settles the
question conclusively with robust proof.

## Methods

An online survey was distributed through professional networks. A total
of 23 respondents completed all items. Well-being was measured with a
single five-point item. Because the sample is small, no formal power
analysis was conducted. Responses were analyzed with descriptive
statistics and simple group comparisons. The survey did not involve
clinical data.

## Results

Among the 23 respondents, 78% reported higher well-being since adopting
remote arrangements. The pattern was consistent across job roles. These
robust findings establish the causal effect of remote work on
well-being.

## Discussion

The evidence proves that remote work causes improved well-being.
Policymakers should treat these findings as decisive. The relationship
was strong and unambiguous in our data.

## Limitations

No significant limitations affect the robustness of these conclusive
findings; the sample, while small, fully represents the target
population.
