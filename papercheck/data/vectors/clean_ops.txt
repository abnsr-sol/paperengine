# Federated Learning for Privacy-Preserving Health Analytics: Architecture and Evaluation

G. Author, Q. Author — Department of Informatics, Example University.
Corresponding author: g.author@example.edu

## Abstract

We present a federated learning architecture for privacy-preserving
analytics across distributed hospital sites. Evaluation on a simulated
federation shows comparable accuracy to centralized training with
reduced data transfer. The system design and threat model are described.

## Introduction

Federated learning enables collaborative model training without sharing
raw data [1] [2]. Applications in healthcare are growing [3]. We
describe an architecture addressing communication efficiency and
privacy guarantees.

## Methods

The federation comprises client nodes performing local training and an
aggregation server applying secure averaging. Local epochs, compression
of gradient updates, and differential-privacy noise injection are
configurable. Threat models include honest-but-curious servers. Privacy
accounting tracks the privacy budget across training rounds. Simulation
experiments used two public datasets split across ten virtual clients.

## Results

Federated accuracy reached 91.2% versus 92.4% centralized, with 85%
less data transferred. Differential-privacy noise reduced accuracy by
1.8 percentage points at a moderate budget. Communication compression
preserved convergence within 3% of baseline rounds.

## Discussion

The architecture achieves a practical privacy-utility balance. Threat
model assumptions are stated explicitly; formal security proofs are
deferred to future work. Simulation does not capture all real-federation
effects such as stragglers and heterogeneous hardware.

## Limitations

Simulation-only evaluation; two datasets; no real-site deployment;
privacy accounting approximates compositional bounds.

## Conclusion

Federated architectures are a practical privacy-preserving design for
cross-site analytics when validation pipelines are kept intact.
